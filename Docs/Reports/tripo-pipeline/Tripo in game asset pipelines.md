# Let Tripo Model, Keep SK_Human Rigging

Don't use Tripo's rigging for any character that wears FratRow cosmetics. Use Tripo Studio to model and texture the pieces that sit on top of the body: tops, bottoms, shoes, hair and earrings. Generate them against a render of `SKM_F_Body` in its A-pose bind, then bind them to `SK_Human` with the charkit scripts the project already has. Tripo's rigger got much better in late September 2026, adding hand bones and Mixamo, Unity Humanoid and UE5 naming presets. It still fits a new skeleton to every mesh, though. It publishes no bone list or finger depth, adds no sockets and knows nothing about the 16 body sliders. A Tripo rig would therefore break three things at once: cosmetic bind poses, skin masks keyed to body vertex IDs, and socket attachments. The real question is less "who rigs" than "who owns the body". While the body and skeleton stay ours, charkit's `bind_to_body` copies weights and every shape key from the skin under each vertex of *any* mesh within 15 cm of the body. Its `skin_mask` ray test also works on items that were not grown from the body. What's missing is an ingest stage at the front of the pipeline, not a new rig. This matches how practitioners use Tripo in late 2026: as a fast draft generator for geometry and textures that feeds Blender, where retopology, UVs, baking and rigging are still done by hand. No shipped game has published a postmortem naming Tripo, and one estimate puts cleanup labor at 20–25× the generation fee. Tripo's rigger is worth paying for in three narrow jobs: non-humanoid creatures, throwaway crowd NPCs, and generating Humanoid animation clips. Three operational facts shape the workflow. The Studio subscription does not pay for API use. The official Blender add-on is pinned to an SDK that stops working when Tripo's v2 API shuts down on 31 October 2026. And with Git LFS off, raw Tripo outputs (up to 2M faces, 4K–8K textures) must stay out of the repo.

## Studios treat Tripo as a draft generator that feeds Blender

By October 2026 Tripo has two model families. **H3.1** builds dense meshes of up to 2M polygons ([Tripo: High Detail Model](https://www.tripo3d.ai/features/high-detail-model)). The **Smart Mesh P-series** builds low-poly meshes, and its current **P2.0** (19 Aug 2026) generates **native quads (500–25K faces) or triangles (500–50K)**. P2.0 also "automatically separates body, clothing, and accessories into independent pieces during generation", accepts four-view input, and returns up to four variants at different face counts for one credit cost ([Tripo blog: P2.0](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/tripo-p2-0-preview)). Around the models sit a delighting toggle, **Remove Lighting**, which is on by default for Smart Mesh ([Tripo: AI Texturing](https://www.tripo3d.ai/features/ai-texturing)), plus AI UV unwrapping, three-tier semantic segmentation and the rebuilt rigger. On quality, Tripo is near the top on every source that tested it, whatever that source's bias. It is #1 on the top3d.ai crowd arena, though the operator uses Tripo referral codes ([Sloyd/top3d.ai](https://www.sloyd.ai/blog/ai-3d-model-generator-rankings)). A Meshy employee testing against his own employer found **Tripo beat Rodin 77.9% on single-view untextured geometry** ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-i-stress-tested-3-ai-3d-generators-on-the-same-inputs-what-the-numbers-actually-show)). Its consistent weakness is mesh hygiene. A competitor's H3.1 chair came out at 965,873 faces with fused rails ([Neural4D](https://blog.neural4d.com/comparisons/tripo-3-1-model-review/)). In a retopology test, Hunyuan produced fewer non-manifold edges ([top3d.ai](https://www.top3d.ai/learn/retopology-comparison-2025)).

The most complete documented Tripo character pipeline comes from September 2026. It uses Tripo only for the high-poly mesh and the first retopology pass. Everything after that happens in Blender, Marmoset and Substance: cleanup, UVs, a cage bake, texturing and a **Rigify rig**, before a Unity Humanoid import ([3DSkillUp](https://3dskillup.art/ai-game-character-workflow-tripo-blender-unity/)). So even a Tripo-centered tutorial rigs in Blender. An outsourcing studio's test scored Tripo 3.5/5. It was the only usable character draft, but not recommended "for deformation-ready models or final production meshes" ([VSQUAD](https://vsquad.art/blog/articles/best-ai-3d-generators/)). A 2026 timeline found that none of the year's major generators produces the edge loops a face or shoulder needs to deform ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026)). Props are where AI output ships close to as-is. Static background props are rated "good after review" ([Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/ai-3d-model-generators-game-development-2026/)). One estimate (modeled, not measured) puts human cleanup at **$18–$34.50 per usable asset against $0.75–$1.50 in API fees** ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/3d-model-access/tripo-ai-review/)).

The evidence has two caveats. First, no named shipped game has published a postmortem on its Tripo pipeline. GDC talks in 2025–2026 were presented by vendors, and nearly every comparison article comes from a vendor or reseller. Second, Reddit was out of reach for the research tools, so community sentiment is secondhand.

The open-source **TripoSR** is not part of any of these pipelines. It is MIT-licensed, needs about 6 GB of VRAM and reconstructs a mesh in under 0.5 s on an A100. It outputs **vertex colors by default**, with no PBR, quads, parts or rig ([TripoSR GitHub](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR)). Its MIT-licensed successors, TripoSG, TripoSF and HoloPart, produce shape only and none of them matches the commercial P2.0 ([TripoSG GitHub](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSG)). For FratRow, TripoSR is at most a free offline placeholder generator.

## Tripo's new rigger still builds a new skeleton for every mesh

The API's newest rigger, `v3.0-20260909`, is described as "faster, higher-quality humanoid rigging". It accepts a `spec` of `tripo`, `mixamo`, `unity`, `unreal`, `vrm` or `actorcore`. However, **"the default is unchanged, so v3.0 must be requested explicitly"**: a default call still gets the 2024 biped rigger ([Tripo Developers: Auto Rig](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/docs/animations-rig)). Studio's version, rebuilt between 30 September and mid-October 2026, rigs "full-body and hand bones" with five engine presets. It accepts humanoids up to **100,000 triangles in T-pose or A-pose**, at 20 credits per character ([Tripo blog: Rigging & Animation](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/rigging-animation); [Spatial Insiders](https://spatialinsiders.com/stories/tripo-rigging-animation-text-to-motion)).

No source publishes Tripo's bone count, finger-chain depth, root convention, rest pose or eye and jaw bones. The quality evidence is one structured test from before the update, in May 2026. It found **over-smoothed shoulder and chest weights that made the mesh slump on arm raises**, noisy jaw weights and no facial joints, alongside the cleanest UE retarget of the tools tested ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)). Tripo's own guide tells users to check for "collapsed shoulders, pinched elbows, detached sleeves, stretched hips" ([Tripo blog: VRChat avatar maker](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/vrchat-avatar-maker)).

Weight quality is not the deciding issue, though. The naming preset fixes bone *names*, but an ML rigger places the bones on each mesh separately. That collides with every part of the contract in `design/art/character-pipeline.md`:

| `SK_Human` contract (pipeline doc) | What a Tripo rig provides | What breaks |
|---|---|---|
| One skeleton; "bone names, hierarchy and rest positions change only with a pipeline-doc update" | Bones fitted to each mesh; the preset controls only naming | `CharacterAppearance` re-binds items to bones by name, but the items' bind poses assume `SK_Human`'s rest positions, so they would sit offset (inference from how Unity skinning works) |
| 54 deform bones incl. eyes and 3-segment fingers; importer uses an explicit 54-bone Humanoid map | Hand bones only since Sept 2026; finger depth and eye bones unpublished | Explicit bone map and phone/cup hand poses at risk |
| 7 non-deforming `Socket_*` bones | None | Earrings, HeadTop, Hand and Back items have nothing to parent to |
| 16 shape-key sliders on the body, copied onto every item | No shape keys | Body-type customization disappears |
| Skin masks keyed to `SKM_F_Body` vertex IDs plus a topology hash | A different mesh topology | Every mask is invalid |
| A-pose bind plus a computed T-pose reference | Whatever pose the mesh was generated in | Avatar setup needed per character |

The strongest case for Tripo's rig is real but does not apply to FratRow. Unity Humanoid would still play shared clips on a Tripo-rigged character. StraySpark calls Tripo the "best end-to-end option for indies who don't need close-up facial work." For a game with fixed, non-customizable characters, that would be enough. FratRow's characters are recipes on one body, so the trade fails. The Unity community's long-standing advice points the same way: a shared base skeleton is standard, and you extend it only by *adding* bones ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-do-you-manage-hundreds-of-different-armor-clothing-pieces-on-a-3d-character-on-the-art-side/923955)). Even Tripo concedes that clothing fitting and weight transfer "remain Blender/Unity tasks" ([Tripo blog: add clothes to VRChat avatar](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/vrchat-how-to-add-clothes-to-avatar-easy)).

## charkit already does the hard half of the pipeline

The deciding fact is in `tools/blender/charkit/cosmetic.py`. **`bind_to_body` does not care how an item was made.** For each vertex it finds the nearest body triangle, blends that triangle's bone weights by barycentric coordinates, and builds all 16 slider shape keys from the body's deltas. It also pushes out any vertex that sinks into the shaped body at full slider value. It refuses any mesh with a vertex more than 15 cm from the skin. `skin_mask` casts a ray along each skin normal and marks a vertex covered when the item is within 2.5 cm, and it works on items that were not grown from the body. This is the same feature set a 2026 commercial add-on, RadFit, sells: "same-named garment key" per body shape key, weight copying and covered-skin masking ([RadFit](https://superhivemarket.com/products/radfit--fit-any-outfit-to-any-body)). That independently confirms the design. A Tripo cosmetic therefore needs a new **front end**, not new rigging.

### Generate against the body you already own

Render `SKM_F_Body` in its exact bind A-pose from front, left, back and right. Paint the outfit over those renders with the image models included in paid Studio tiers (Nano Banana, GPT Image 2 and Midjourney ([Tripo pricing](https://www.tripo3d.ai/pricing))). Then feed the views to P2.0 multi-view, which is subscribers-only, with Remove Lighting on. From there you can either **generate the garment alone** from an "invisible mannequin" image, or **generate the whole dressed character and split it** with P2.0's automatic part separation or with Segmentation v2, whose `ref_image` parameter can steer the split ([Tripo API: Mesh Segmentation](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/docs/mesh-segment.md)). Either way, **throw away the AI body**. Meshy admits the body under a coat is "a plausible guess" that gets worse with every layer ([Meshy](https://www.meshy.ai/topics/can-a-selfie-become-a-3d-character)). Matching the bind pose matters because pose mismatch is the most common documented cause of failed weight transfer. Transferring from a posed source "doubles up" deformation ([Blender DevTalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/please-give-option-to-transfer-weights-from-a-posed-mesh-without-doubling-up-deforms/4564)).

### Ingest stage, then the existing scripts

The new stage normalizes each Tripo mesh to the project's export conventions: 1 m units, a 1.66 m character, facing −Y, feet at Z = 0. It then aligns the mesh to the body. Segmented parts come back as closed shells with AI-guessed insides, so it deletes the inner shell and any fused body remnants. Next it reduces the mesh to the doc's budgets: **hair ≤ 1,500 tris, and top, bottom and shoes ≤ 3,000 combined**. Picking a low P2.0 variant usually covers this. It then UV-unwraps with Smart UV (input ≤ 80,000 equivalent triangles ([tripo-python-sdk API](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/tripo-python-sdk/blob/master/docs/API.md))) or in Blender, and bakes Tripo's detail into a normal map. It converts the albedo to luminance plus a region mask so the property-block tints keep working. Finally it conforms the mesh to the body with a Shrinkwrap offset and the existing 3 mm `ensure_clearance`.

From there the current path runs unchanged: `bind_to_body`, `skin_mask`, then `review.mask_preview` and `review.preview_variants` with every slider maxed and the test poses, then export.

| Slot | Tripo route | charkit path | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top / Bottom / FullBody | P2.0 multi-view on base-body renders, or a split dressed character | ingest → `bind_to_body` → `skin_mask` | Loose fabric (skirts, wide sleeves) takes weights from the nearest skin, which pulls the cloth onto the legs and arms |
| Shoes | P2.0 garment alone | ingest → `bind_to_body` | Sole thickness against the clearance pass |
| Hair | P2.0, kept as a solid "helmet" shape | ingest → head/neck weights | Long ponytails exceed the 15 cm guard and abort the bind |
| Earrings, HeadTop, Hand, Back | P2.0 at minimum face count | rigid, parented to `Socket_*`; no weights | Scale and pivot only; the easiest first win |

AI hair arrives as "one dense, watertight mesh", not as hair cards ([Tripo blog: hair cards](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/ai-3d-model-generator-converting-hair-cards-for-games)). For a ~6k-tri stylized body, retopologized helmet hair is the cheap path.

### Use body-grown shells for tight garments

For tight garments (crop tops, leggings, socks), the body-grown shell is still the better base mesh. It keeps exact masks through `COVERED_PROP` and guarantees the fit. Tripo can still supply the *look*: bake the Tripo garment's normals and albedo onto the shell. Save Tripo's full geometry for shape-heavy items the shell method can't grow, such as shoes, hair, accessories and structured or puffy garments. No source documents this hybrid; it is an inference from the bake-from-high-poly practice above.

### Two known weak spots

**First, loose garments.** Nearest-skin transfer fails between the legs and under the arms. Robust Weight Transfer fixes this by inpainting the rejected vertices ([GitHub: robust-weight-transfer](https://github.com/sentfromspacevr/robust-weight-transfer)). It supported only Blender 2.9–4.1 as of 2025, though, and charkit targets Blender 5.0 ([CGDive](https://cgdivetest.super.site/tools/robust-weight-transfer)). Either verify it on 5.0 or port the inpainting step into `bind_to_body`.

**Second, masks.** AI items have no `COVERED_PROP` to limit the ray test. The docstring's own example applies: a sleeve rising over a bare shoulder would wrongly hide skin. Derive an allowed vertex set from the `BR_*` region slots instead.

## Tripo's rigger is worth its credits in three jobs

**Non-humanoid creatures** are Tripo's real differentiator. Its v2.5 rigger handles quadrupeds, hexapods, serpents and more ([Tripo Developers: Auto Rig](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/docs/animations-rig)), and Meshy's rigging is humanoid-only ([Meshy API docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/rigging-and-animation)). A campus dog or mascot should be rigged by Tripo, because `SK_Human` can't serve it anyway.

**Throwaway crowd NPCs** that never change clothes or body shape can use Tripo rigs too. Even so, the modular system already makes NPC variety cheap through recipes. That keeps one avatar and one material scheme, and it's usually the better choice.

**Generating animation clips** is the most interesting use. Rig a throwaway proxy character once, with v3.0 and the Unity Humanoid or Mixamo preset. Pull clips from Studio's free preset library, or generate Text to Motion clips at 20 credits each, which run 1–10 s and chain up to five segments ([Tripo blog: Rigging & Animation](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/rigging-animation)). Export the clips as FBX and import them as Humanoid animation, exactly like the Mixamo clips today. The proxy never ships; only its motion does. Mixamo's library is "from a different era and look[s] subtly dated" ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ai-auto-rigging-showdown-2026-tripo-meshy-cascadeur-mixamo)), so dances and party gestures are worth testing here.

Several things about this route are unverified. Nobody has checked whether the clips carry finger curves, what root-motion convention or frame rate they use, or what licence terms cover them. Text to Motion is Studio-only: the SDK's 1.0 release removed it ([tripo-python-sdk commits](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/tripo-python-sdk/commits/master)). Tripo's own retargeting rejects Mixamo-spec rigs made with v1.0 ([ComfyUI: TripoRetargetNode](https://docs.comfy.org/built-in-nodes/TripoRetargetNode)), so the proxy must use v3.0.

One more experiment is possible: rig with Tripo, rename the vertex groups to `SK_Human` names, and compare those weights with body transfer on fused garments. Nobody has measured whether that wins, so it is low priority.

## Billing, an API deadline and a lean repo shape the daily workflow

**Studio and API are billed separately**: "If I have a Tripo Studio subscription, can I use the API service? No." ([Tripo developer portal](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/)). Studio's per-step costs are:

| Step | Studio credits |
|---|---|
| P2.0 mesh | 100 |
| Smart UV | 20 |
| Texture | 20 |
| Rigging | 20 |
| Mesh Edit (after 3 free per model on Pro) | 50 |

([Tripo blog: End-to-End 3D Pipeline](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/3d-game-pipeline)). Skipping rigging, a cosmetic costs about 140 credits. **Pro's 3,000 monthly credits ($20/mo billed annually) therefore cover about 21 cosmetics before retries, and Max's 25,000 cover about 178** ([Tripo pricing](https://www.tripo3d.ai/pricing)). Check which tier the team actually bought.

Automation is a separate pay-as-you-go balance at 100 credits per $1, and API credits never expire. **The v2 API shuts down on 2026-10-31**, so pin `tripo3d>=1.0.0,<2.0.0` ([tripo-python-sdk](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/tripo-python-sdk)). The official Blender add-on bundles a copy of the SDK frozen at a commit from 2025-10-23, so expect it to break after the cutoff ([tripo-3d-for-blender](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/tripo-3d-for-blender)). Studio's DCC Bridge doesn't depend on the API version, so it is the safer route from Studio into Blender. The repo's own `generate_3d` MCP tool reaches Tripo through mcp-for-blender's own key path, not the Studio credits. Download URLs expire after five minutes, so scripts must save outputs immediately.

With Git LFS disabled, raw downloads belong in a gitignored folder or external storage. Commit only the low-poly FBX, the compressed `.blend` and downsized textures. Tripo's own advice is that "an 8K source is not automatically an 8K runtime requirement" ([Tripo blog: P2.0 game character](https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/mesh-game-character-p-2)).

**Commercial use needs a paid account.** Tripo's terms say it "retains all rights" to Free users' outputs, while Paid users "generally have all rights" ([Tripo Terms](https://www.tripo3d.ai/terms)). Read literally, assets generated on Free shouldn't ship.

Steam treats shipped AI-made artwork as **Pre-Generated AI content you must "describe … in detail"** ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). Add a provenance field to each `.cosmetic.json` sidecar now: generator, model version, reference source and cleanup done.

For hard-surface props that need clean topology, Rodin Gen-2.5 or Hunyuan 3D 3.1 are the better pairing ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-asset-generators-games)). Avoid Hunyuan3D 2.1's open weights: their licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea ([HF licence](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)).

## Conclusion

The project has already decided this question. The reason the pipeline doc bans Mixamo's auto-rigger is that "it replaces `SK_Human` and drops finger weights, sockets and shape keys". That applies word for word to Tripo's rigger, which is a better auto-rigger aimed at the same slot. Widen the ban to "any auto-rigger", with the creature, crowd and animation-clip exceptions written down, so the next tool's marketing doesn't reopen it. What Tripo changes is the *top* layer of the stack. Cosmetics that used to be grown procedurally or modeled by hand can now come from an image. The skeleton, body, sliders and masks stay the fixed base that makes them interchangeable.

The open risk is cleanup time, not rigging. Nobody has measured how many minutes a Tripo garment needs before `bind_to_body` accepts it. That number decides whether Tripo beats the body-grown script path for each slot. Run the cheap experiments in order of risk. Start with one earring (rigid, no weights) and one pair of shoes. Then try one loose skirt, which exercises the weak nearest-skin transfer and the mask without `COVERED_PROP`. Time each one. That data, not vendor benchmarks, should set how much of the wardrobe Tripo builds.
